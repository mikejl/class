PART 1 · FOUNDATION
Virtualization: one server, many machines
A hypervisor carves one physical server into many isolated virtual machines (VMs).
Hypervisor
Shares out CPU, memory, storage, and network. Examples: VMware ESXi, Hyper-V, KVM.
Isolation
Each VM runs its own OS and apps. A crash in one doesn't take down the others.
VMs are files
Copy, snapshot, or move them to new hardware in minutes.

PART 1 · FOUNDATION
From virtualization to cloud
Cloud = virtualized resources + automation + self-service, delivered over a network.
NIST's five essential characteristics of cloud computing
01
On-demand self-service
Spin up a server from a web portal. No ticket, no waiting.
02
Broad network access
Reach it from laptops, phones, and other devices over the network.
03
Resource pooling
Many customers share the provider's virtualized hardware.
04
Rapid elasticity
Add or remove capacity in minutes, often automatically.
05
Measured service
Usage is metered, so you pay for what you use.

SERVICE MODEL 1 OF 3
Software as a Service (SaaS)
Finished applications delivered over the internet, usually by a third party. Open a browser or app, sign in, and use it.
YOU MANAGE
Your data, your users, and settings
PROVIDER MANAGES
The app, servers, updates, and patches
Pizza analogy: dining out. You just eat.
EXAMPLES
Microsoft 365
Outlook, Word, and Teams in the browser
Google Workspace
Gmail, Docs, and Drive
Salesforce
Customer relationship management (CRM)
Zoom
Video meetings with nothing to host

SERVICE MODEL 2 OF 3
Platform as a Service (PaaS)
A ready-made platform (runtime, database, developer tools) for building and deploying your own apps. No servers or OS to manage.
YOU MANAGE
Your code, your data, and app settings
PROVIDER MANAGES
Runtime, OS, servers, scaling, and patches
Pizza analogy: delivery. You set the table.
EXAMPLES
Heroku
Push your code and it runs
Google App Engine
Hosts web apps and APIs at scale
AWS Elastic Beanstalk
Deploys and scales web apps on AWS
Azure App Service
Hosts web apps, APIs, and mobile back ends

SERVICE MODEL 3 OF 3
Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS)
Pay-as-you-go virtual servers, storage, and networking. The provider runs the data center; you build everything on top.
YOU MANAGE
OS, patches, firewall rules, apps, and data
PROVIDER MANAGES
Physical hardware, virtualization, facility
Pizza analogy: take-and-bake. You supply the oven.
EXAMPLES
Amazon EC2
Virtual servers billed by the second or hour
Azure Virtual Machines
Windows and Linux VMs on demand
Google Compute Engine
VMs on Google's global network
Amazon S3
Storage, paid per gigabyte stored

PART 3 · CAPACITY
Capacity: fixed vs. elastic
On-prem: buy for the peak
Hardware sized for the busiest day sits idle most of the year. Size it lower, and the spike causes slowdowns or outages.
Cloud: match the demand
Auto-scaling adds servers as traffic climbs and removes them when it drops. You pay for what you use.

PART 4 · IOT AND MOBILE
Mobile: the phone's cloud back end
Your phone holds the screen. The cloud holds the data, the identity, and the heavy computing.
Sync and backup
Photos and files copy to iCloud or Google Photos and show up on every device.
Sign-in and identity
One account works across your phone, laptop, and the web.
Push notifications
Cloud services wake your app with messages and alerts.
Heavy lifting
Voice assistants, map routing, and translation run on cloud servers.
Developers often build these with mobile back-end platforms such as Firebase and AWS Amplify (a PaaS flavor).

RECAP
Key takeaways
01
Virtualization is the foundation
Hypervisors turn one server into many VMs; the cloud pools them and rents them out.
02
Service models split the work
IaaS: you run the OS and up. PaaS: you bring the code. SaaS: you just use it.
03
Cloud capacity follows demand
Elastic and pay-as-you-go, instead of buying hardware for the peak.
04
IoT and mobile run on the cloud
It supplies the storage, sync, analytics, and control that small devices can't.
