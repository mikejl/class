Quantum gates are rotations of the sphere

A classical NOT gate flips a bit. Quantum gates do something richer: each one is a 2×2 matrix that turns the qubit's vector, which on the Bloch sphere means rotating the arrow around an axis. Every gate can be undone, and none of them destroys information.

04 · Two qubits

CNOT: the gate that connects qubits

The controlled-NOT acts on two qubits. If the first (control) qubit is |1⟩, it flips the second (target). Otherwise it does nothing. Because two qubits have four basis states, CNOT is a 4×4 matrix.

CNOT (controlled-NOT, also CX) — the standard two-qubit gate. Together with single-qubit rotations it can build any quantum circuit. When the control is in superposition, CNOT creates entanglement.

05 · Security connection

Reversible by design

Every quantum gate is unitary, meaning it can be run backward to recover the input exactly. Classical cryptographic hash functions are deliberately the opposite: they throw information away. Quantum algorithms like Shor's still evaluate math functions, but they must do it with reversible circuits, which is part of why they need so many qubits.

Unitary — a matrix U whose conjugate transpose U† is its inverse (U†U = I). Unitary operations preserve the total probability of 1 and can always be undone.
